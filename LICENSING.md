This repository documentation is intended to explain the project's licensing
model and is not legal advice. The copyright holder should obtain professional

## 1. Licensing overview

Energy System Simulator is source-available under the Business Source License
1.1 and commercially licensed for private commercial production use.

The public licence permits personal, educational, academic, research,
evaluation, testing, development, and other non-production uses. Private
commercial production use requires a separate paid commercial licence from the
copyright holder.

The current public licence is not an OSI-approved open source licence. Each
released version converts to the Apache License 2.0 on its applicable Change
Date.

## 2. Uses allowed without payment

The following uses are allowed under the BSL 1.1 terms without payment:

- Reading and studying the source code.
- Personal experimentation.
- Educational use and university teaching.
- Academic and research use.
- Evaluation and testing.
- Development and other non-production use.
- Local company evaluation before deciding whether to buy a commercial licence.

A company may inspect and evaluate the software without payment when that use is
limited to evaluation, testing, development, or another non-production purpose.

## 3. Uses requiring a commercial licence

A paid commercial licence is required for private commercial production use,
including:

- Internal production deployment.
- Internal business operations.
- Proprietary product integration.
- Paid client projects.
- Hosted, managed, SaaS, or platform services.
- Commercial redistribution.
- Revenue-generating or operational commercial activity.

Merely keeping modifications private does not remove the commercial licensing
requirement when the use is private commercial production use.

## 5. Change Date and Apache 2.0 conversion

The Apache 2.0 conversion applies separately to each released version. A
version's Change Date is exactly four calendar years after that version's
release date, as recorded in `licensing/releases.json`.

Newer versions may remain under BSL 1.1 after older versions convert. Rights
already granted under Apache 2.0 after conversion cannot be withdrawn for the
converted version.

Release entries must include:

- `version`
- `release_date`
- `change_date`
- `change_license`

The expected `change_license` value is `Apache-2.0`.

## 7. Frequently asked questions

### Can I use this for personal projects?

Yes, personal use is allowed under the public BSL 1.1 terms.

### Can a company evaluate the software?

Yes, a company may inspect, test, and evaluate the software without payment when
the use is non-production.

### Can my company run it in production without a paid licence?

No. Private commercial production use requires a separate paid commercial
licence unless the applicable version has reached its Change Date.

### What happens on the Change Date?

On the applicable Change Date, that released version converts to Apache License
2.0. The conversion applies to that version only.

### Are newer versions automatically Apache 2.0 when older versions convert?

No. Newer versions may remain under BSL 1.1 until their own Change Dates.
